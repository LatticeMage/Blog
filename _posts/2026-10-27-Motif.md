I was having a conversation with a friend the other day, and we stumbled onto a really fascinating pattern.

Have you ever noticed that whether it’s MMA, music, or the movies, anime, and books we consume—once you reach the absolute pinnacle of any discipline, **the lines between different "styles" and "media" just completely vanish?**

Most people think top performers are just competing over who has the "strongest signature move." But in reality, the absolute monsters in these fields aren't even playing the same game as everyone else.

---

## 1. Fighting and Music: When You Stop Playing the Standard Board Game

We were talking about MMA legends like Demetrious "Mighty Mouse" Johnson, Georges St-Pierre (GSP), and Jon "Bones" Jones.

Back in the day, fight fans loved arguing over style match-ups: *"Can Muay Thai beat Wrestling?"* or *"Can Jiu-Jitsu counter Judo?"* But guys like Mighty Mouse completely shattered that way of thinking. His defining magic is transitioning seamlessly—like catching a guy mid-air off a suplex and locking in an armbar before they even hit the canvas. **To him, there is no separation between "striking mode" and "grappling mode." There is only the fluid dynamics of momentum and exploiting openings.**

They play a game called **"Forced Discomfort"**: instead of clashing head-on in an area where you're strong, they use seamless transitions to drag you into a position where you are at your absolute worst.

Over in the music world, there’s a similar alien-level talent: **Jacob Collier**.

People often joke that Jacob’s music theory operates a dimension above the rest of the planet. Traditional musicians work within the 12 discrete notes of equal temperament. To Jacob, sound is a continuous spectrum. He can micro-shift the pitch of a song by 4.5 cents mid-track, tugging at your emotions without your brain even consciously realizing what changed.

**Mighty Mouse turned MMA into fluid mechanics, and Jacob turned music into multidimensional geometry.** They aren't just making great moves within the rules—they see straight through the underlying physics of the game and rewrite the rules altogether.

---

## 2. The Audience’s Evolution: From Superficial Hype to Core Motifs

What's really cool is that this "dissolution of boundaries" doesn't just happen to elite performers; it happens to us as consumers of art and storytelling, too.

Think back to when you first started getting into novels, anime, games, or films:

* **At first, we’re hooked by surface elements:** *"Whoa, super powers!"*, *"Giant robots!"*, *"Awesome art style!"*
* **After seeing enough, we start appreciating technique:** We pay attention to the director's camera work, non-linear storytelling, or harmonic layers in a soundtrack.
* **Eventually, medium boundaries disappear entirely.** You stop caring whether a story is told through animation, live-action film, or pages in a book. You start hunting for a **cross-medium "Motif."**

For instance, if you realize you are equally obsessed with the anime *Ghost in the Shell*, the movie *Blade Runner*, the video game *Detroit: Become Human*, and the quiet, lonely paintings of Edward Hopper...

You aren't actually obsessed with "sci-fi" or "androids." What you’ve been chasing all along is the core motif: **the ultimate isolation of the modern human experience and the search for identity in a crowded world.**

---

## The Takeaway: Art as a Mirror

From mixed martial arts to microtonal music, and all the way to the stories we fall in love with, the journey of mastery and appreciation follows the exact same curve:

> **Beginners look at techniques and surface tropes. Masters and seasoned audiences look at game-state control and underlying motifs.**

When you stop letting superficial labels define what you watch, listen to, or practice, you start seeing the underlying skeleton beneath it all. And in doing so, you don't just understand art better—you finally get a clear look at what your own soul has been searching for all along.

That, to me, is what makes watching the world unfold so incredibly fun.
